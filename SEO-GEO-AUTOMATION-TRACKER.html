Which work is SEO, which is GEO — and why

A plain-terms breakdown of the tasks completed so far, for Kenneth's reporting use.

SEO (Search Engine Optimization) = making your website show up well in classic Google search — the list of blue links people click through.

GEO (Generative / AI-Search Optimization) = making your website understandable and trustworthy to AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overviews — so when someone asks an AI "what's a good centre management app in Singapore," the AI can find EdBuddies, describe it correctly, and recommend it instead of getting confused or skipping it.

They overlap a lot — a lot of what helps Google also helps AI tools — but not always.

Task-by-task breakdown

TaskTypeWhy it helps (plain terms)
Full site audit SEO + GEO Found the problems before fixing anything blind.
Decide how pages load Both — the biggest one Your site was JavaScript-only, meaning Google (on first pass) and almost every AI tool saw a blank page for every URL except the homepage. This is the single most important fix on this whole list.
Which pages should/shouldn't show in search SEO Stops internal draft pages from confusing Google or diluting the real pages.
Page speed check SEO Slow pages rank worse in Google; less relevant to AI tools.
Page titles & descriptions SEO This is literally the blue link + snippet text people see in Google results.
Canonical links, sitemap, robots.txt SEO Housekeeping that tells Google exactly which pages exist and which one is the "real" version.
Structured data (hidden info tags: "this is a company," "this is an FAQ," "this is a breadcrumb trail") GEO-heavy Written specifically for machines, not humans. AI tools lean on this heavily to pull out facts confidently. Classic Google also uses some of it for the little extra info boxes in results.
Fixing heading structure (one clear title per page) Both Google uses headings to understand what a page is about; AI tools use the same structure to summarise a page correctly.
Image descriptions (alt text) SEO (minor) Helps Google Images and screen readers; not really an AI-chat factor.
Fixing broken/wrong internal links SEO Google follows your links to discover pages; broken ones waste that.
Fixing "page not found" behaviour SEO Google was seeing every broken link as "this page is fine" (200) instead of "this page doesn't exist" (404) — confusing, and can hurt trust in the whole site.
Building the actual working version of the page-loading fix Both This is where a bug was caught that would've made Google stop showing the site entirely — a good example of why testing matters.
Matching search terms to the right page SEO Makes sure two pages aren't competing against each other for the same Google search.
Rewriting homepage/feature-page wording for search terms SEO Makes the wording match what people actually type into Google.
Internal "facts sheet" about what EdBuddies actually is GEO Doesn't go on the website — it's a reference so any future AI-search work stays factually consistent, since AI tools get confused (and less confident/accurate) when a company describes itself differently in different places.
Making FAQs directly answer the question (instead of "contact us to find out") GEO-heavy AI tools copy/extract direct answers. A vague non-answer gives them nothing useful to quote.
Calling EdBuddies the same thing everywhere ("app," not sometimes "platform") GEO-heavy AI tools build a mental model of what the product is — inconsistent wording makes that model fuzzier and less trustworthy.
Competitor-claims safety rule Risk mgmt Stops an AI (or Google) from ever surfacing an unapproved or inaccurate comparison claim about a competitor.
Automation permission rules Neither Process/governance only — defines how Claude is allowed to work going forward, not something that affects rankings or AI visibility.
The short version:
  • SEO work = making sure Google can find, correctly read, and fairly rank every real page on the site (titles, links, speed, page structure).
  • GEO work = making sure AI tools can correctly understand and confidently describe EdBuddies when someone asks them about it (structured data, direct answers, consistent facts and wording).
  • The single biggest win covers both: the site was invisible to almost every non-Chrome-style crawler because it needed JavaScript to show anything. Fixing that is the foundation everything else builds on.
